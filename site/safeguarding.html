01 · Our Safeguarding Commitment

Safe places to play and grow.

Sporting Pearls Football Academy is committed to protecting children and young people involved in its programmes from abuse, neglect, exploitation, bullying, harassment, discrimination and other forms of harm.

Our safeguarding policy establishes the principles, responsibilities and procedures through which we prevent safeguarding risks, promote a culture of safety, respond appropriately to concerns, provide safe reporting channels, and work appropriately with parents, guardians and relevant authorities.

It applies to everyone involved with the academy — players, coaches, staff, volunteers, board members, parents and guardians, visitors and partners — during training, matches, tournaments, travel, camps, academy events, community activities, digital communication and any activity conducted in Sporting Pearls' name.

Core Principle "The welfare and best interests of the child come before football performance, competition, reputation or organizational interests."

02 · Our Principles

Eight principles. One standard.

Every coach, volunteer, staff member and leader at Sporting Pearls works to the same safeguarding principles.

1

Child First

Children's welfare takes priority.

2

Prevention

We seek to identify and reduce risks before harm occurs.

3

Listening

Children should have safe and appropriate opportunities to express concerns.

4

Respect

Every child deserves dignity and fair treatment.

5

Accountability

Adults working with children are responsible for their conduct.

6

Confidentiality

Safeguarding information will be handled responsibly and shared only where appropriate and necessary.

7

Non-Retaliation

A person raising a genuine concern should not be punished or intimidated for reporting it.

8

Best Interests

Actions concerning a child should prioritise their safety and wellbeing.

03 · How to Raise a Concern

Concerned about a child?

Anyone can raise a concern — a player, parent, guardian, coach, volunteer or visitor. Use our confidential online form, write to the Safeguarding Lead, or speak to any member of academy staff. You do not need proof, and you may report anonymously.

Report the concern. Don't wait for proof.If you are concerned about a child's safety, report it. You do not need to be certain — every concern is taken seriously.
Open the Confidential Report Form
1

Listen

Allow the person to communicate without unnecessary questioning.

2

Reassure

Respond calmly and take the concern seriously.

3

Record

Document the information accurately and promptly.

4

Report

Pass the concern to the designated safeguarding lead through the established procedure.

5

Escalate

Where necessary, the safeguarding lead refers the matter to appropriate external authorities or professional services.

What our staff and volunteers will never do

  • ✕Promise absolute secrecy
  • ✕Investigate allegations themselves
  • ✕Confront an alleged perpetrator
  • ✕Conduct unnecessary interviews
  • ✕Pressure a child for details
  • ✕Delay reporting because they are uncertain
  • ✕Delete relevant messages or evidence
  • ✕Attempt to resolve serious concerns informally

The purpose of academy staff is to recognise, respond, record and report — not to conduct professional investigations.

In an emergency

Immediate serious risk — the priority is
Get the child to safety→Seek appropriate emergency help→Notify the safeguarding lead→Document the incident

Where a child appears to face an immediate serious risk, emergency help comes first. Our emergency procedure uses verified local emergency and child-protection contacts — we do not publish unverified telephone numbers.

04 · Recruitment & Vetting

Positions of trust.

Adults working with young people hold positions of trust — so safeguarding checks are built into recruitment.

Depending on the role, joining the Sporting Pearls team involves a structured screening process. No one is considered suitable simply because they have football experience.

"No person should automatically be considered suitable simply because they have football experience."

  • Application form and identity verification
  • References and relevant qualifications
  • Safeguarding training
  • Appropriate background / vetting checks
  • Interview
  • Code-of-conduct acknowledgement
  • Declaration of safeguarding responsibilities
Code of Conduct — Adults Working With Players

Clear boundaries, always.

  • Treat players respectfully and maintain appropriate boundaries
  • Never humiliate, degrade or use inappropriate language
  • Avoid favouritism and never use physical punishment
  • Never exploit a position of authority
  • Follow communication, photography and media procedures
  • Report safeguarding concerns and respect confidentiality

05 · Player & Parent Information

Everyone knows their part.

Safeguarding is a shared responsibility. Here is what we expect of our players — and what parents and guardians can expect from us.

For Players

Our player code of conduct keeps every squad safe, fair and enjoyable. Players are expected to:

  • Respect teammates, opponents, coaches and officials
  • Reject bullying and discriminatory behaviour
  • Report concerns and protect teammates
  • Follow reasonable safety instructions

For Parents & Guardians

Parents and guardians receive safeguarding information and know exactly who to approach with a concern:

  • Safeguarding information and relevant codes of conduct
  • Reporting procedures and emergency information
  • Consent information and communication expectations
  • A named person to approach with any concern
"Player welfare comes before social-media engagement."

Before any identifiable image of a young player is published, appropriate consent must be in place. Clear rules govern who may photograph players, where images may be used, and how consent is recorded — and images are never used in a manner that compromises a child's dignity, safety or privacy.

06 · Safeguarding Contact

Talk to someone.

Designated Focal Point

Welfare & Safeguarding Lead

Receives safeguarding concerns, coordinates initial responses, escalates serious concerns to the appropriate authorities, and maintains the academy's safeguarding records and policies.

Sporting Pearls Football Academy · Mtwapa, Kilifi County, Kenya
Report Online · Confidential

The fastest way to raise a concern. Our confidential online form reaches the academy's safeguarding team directly, is logged with a reference number, and lets you choose between confidential follow-up or a completely anonymous report.

In line with our own policy, we only publish verified contact details. The Safeguarding Lead's direct line will be published here once confirmed — until then, the confidential form and the academy office are the ways to reach the safeguarding team.

If a child is in immediate danger, contact the police or appropriate emergency services first, then notify the academy.
